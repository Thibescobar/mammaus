IN BRIEF
--------
This model is an artificial intelligence tool capable of analyzing breast ultrasound images and classifying them into three categories: benign, malignant, or normal.

It was trained on approximately 1,500 ultrasound images from 600 female patients aged 25 to 75. Its architecture is based on a Vision Transformer (ViT), a neural network type particularly suited for medical image analysis.

In tests on images it had never seen, the model achieved an overall accuracy of 96%, with a malignant case detection rate of 94%. These results are promising, but the model remains an assistive tool: it does not replace the expertise of a radiologist and may make mistakes, especially on images of variable quality or from devices different from those used for training.

The model distinguishes three categories:
  • Benign  — a mass or abnormality is visible, but it is not cancerous (e.g., cyst, fibroadenoma).
  • Malignant  — a suspicious mass is detected, possibly corresponding to cancer.
  • Normal — the breast tissue appears healthy, with no mass or particular abnormality.
The key difference between "benign" and "normal": benign means something is visible (a lesion), but it is not cancerous; normal means there is nothing particular to report. In practice, in medical AI, the boundary between these two classes is blurry: normal images are sometimes labeled benign and vice versa in the training data. Therefore, "benign" and "normal" should be considered as two categories likely to be confused by the model. Moreover, the breast often contains benign structures, so a truly "normal" appearance is relatively rare. In contrast, the "malignant" class is much more discriminative.

ARCHITECTURE
------------
  Base        : google/vit-base-patch16-224-in21k
                (Vision Transformer — ViT)
  Patches     : 16 × 16 pixels
  Input       : image 224 × 224 pixels (auto-resized)
  Modification: final pooling layer replaced by a linear classifier (768 → 3 classes, 3 biases)
  The rest of the ViT architecture is unchanged.

OUTPUT CLASSES
--------------
  0 — benign     : non-cancerous appearance
  1 — malignant  : suspicious/cancerous appearance
  2 — normal     : no visible abnormality

INTENDED USE
------------
  • Classification of clean breast ultrasound images
  • Users: radiologists, oncologists, researchers
  • Integration: research pipelines, decision support tools, web demos (Gradio/Streamlit)

OUT OF SCOPE
------------
  • Mammography, MRI, CT — ultrasound only
  • Images with annotations, calipers, overlaid text
  • Autonomous diagnosis without radiologist review
  • Pediatric cases or non-breast ultrasounds
  • Multi-lesional or ambiguous cases

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
-----------------
  • Small training dataset (~1,500 images)
  • No guarantee of worldwide representativity (ethnicity, breast density, ultrasound equipment)
  • Only 3 classes — no nuance (BI-RADS, etc.)
  • Variable performance depending on image quality, acquisition device, and population
  • Possible false negatives → risk of missed diagnosis
  • Possible false positives → unnecessary exams

ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS
----------------------
  • Potential bias due to limited dataset
  • Always combine with expert clinical opinion
  • Comply with local regulations (GDPR, etc.) for patient data use
  • External clinical validation recommended before any deployment
